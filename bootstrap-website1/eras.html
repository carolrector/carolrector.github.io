Album Reviews

Rating: 9/10

Cinematic, dramatic, and emotionally intense. This album defined Lana’s identity and introduced her signature themes of love, fame, and melancholy.

This album is what got me hooked. I absolutely adore the popular songs, "Born to Die", "Blue Jeans", "Video Games", "National Anthem", "Radio", and "Summertime Sadness", as most of the mainstream music listening community does. I think this album might be her most publicly loved album with so many chart-topping hits it's hard to fathom.

I also found love for "Million Dollar Man", "Lucky Ones", "Without You", and "Diet Mountain Dew" after diving deeper, though they are so different in tone. It definitely took me a little more time to get into the style of these songs, but now I can't imagine not liking them!

I personally think that "Lolita", "Off To The Races", "This Is What Makes Us Girls", and "Carmen" are a little out of my style, hence the slightly lowered score, but I'll still give them a listen every once and a while!

Overall, I think this is my second favorite album of Lana's, but it is such a close call to make.

Rating: 8/10

Darker and more rock-inspired, this album leans into raw emotion and stripped-back production.

This album is much moodier, definitely not the same dream-land mood you are in when you listen to Born To Die, but I absolutely love this side of Lana. Her voice quality really shines with these long, sad, reminiscent songs.

My personal favorites here are "Cruel World", "Shades of Cool", "Brooklyn Baby", "West Coast", "Black Beauty", and "Florida Kilos". Again, lots of really great popular songs that still struck a cord with mainstream audiences.

I really don't have many songs that I would skip here, they're all a similar vibe, perfect when you're in the mood. When I got into this album, this is where I started to learn that she loves to cover very old songs from the 1900s, like "The Other Woman" and I just find that fascinating. I love that she is giving these songs a new life and pushing them to a younger generation that definitely has the same feelings, maybe just in a new context.

Rating: 8/10

Dreamy, cinematic, and slow-burning. A very atmospheric and aesthetic-focused project.

I find this album to be a lot more melancholy and slow than some of the others, but that is just what Lana sings so well. My favorite tracks are definitely "Religion", "The Blackest Day", "Art Deco", "High By The Beach", and "Salvatore".

I tend to want to skip the "Burnt Norton Interlude" and "24" but each time I do listen to them they pleasantly suprise me. Definitely not my most favorite album of Lana's, but as always, I love them all.

Rating: 9/10

A brighter, more collaborative album that mixes Lana’s classic sadness with moments of hope.

I cannot get over the beautiful, warm, summery, vibe that Lana mixes here with her sad undertones and gritty desire I think it is such a unique feel and really shows what an artist Lana is and lets her voice quality shine.

I love every single track on this album. I would have to say though that the song I am in most awe of is "Heroin". I have never heard anything like it, it makes me feel like I'm floating into space in a dream scene of a movie. Chills!

This one is definitely a contender for my favorite Lana album, but I think NFR takes the cake just barely. This one by far is my favorite summer listen, though. Can't beat the vibes.

Rating: 10/10

Lana at her lyrical peak—mature, introspective, and beautifully written.

Hard to say for sure, but I think this is my favorite Lana album of all time. I do not skip a single song.

So many mainstream-loved songs here for a reason. I actually didn't even know that "Doin' Time" was a Sublime cover for the longest time, and to me this will always be the superior version.

"Bartender" is my favorite song on this album and it is so underrated. It is so unique and captures the feelings it talks about so well. You just have to listen to feel it.

10/10 no notes.

Rating: 8/10

Soft, introspective, and more minimal—focused on identity and personal reflection.

This album feels a lot more wild, free, and stripped-down to me. I love seeing this vibe coming into her music more recently too.

I would say my favorite tracks are definitely "Yosemite", "Chemtrails Over the Country Club", and "Dark But Just A Game". "Breaking Up Slowly" with Nikki Lane also did a number on me when I was graduating high school. Tear jerker!

Lana's older music will always be my favorite, but this album still has a special place in my heart.

Rating: 7/10

Very personal and stripped down, focusing on storytelling and emotional honesty.

This might come in as my least favorite album, but that doesn't mean that there aren't at least 3 songs that I absolutely adore.

I think "Dealer", "Thunder", and "Living Legend" are three of the saddest songs I've ever heard in my life! Lana's wails in "Dealer" will never not be insane. I don't know how she does it. "Thunder" immediately brings tears to my eyes even with just the first melody, and "Living Legend" somehow brings me straight into Lana's lived experience.

So much power in such a stripped-down vibe.

Rating: 8.5/10

Deeply vulnerable, poetic, and experimental—one of her most complex works.

I love the new sounds, ad libs, features, and tracks she added with this album, it absolutely stunned me when it first came out.

"Let The Light In" and "Margaret" have to be my favorite here. They just paint the picture of what Lana sings about so well, it makes me feel like I'm there. I also love that she reprised her own song "Venice B*tch" and mashed it with a new one, it made for such a new kind of song organization while keeping her familiar tone. So cool.
